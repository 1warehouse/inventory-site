Why people use it

So, next time…

At home

  • You're in the shop wondering if you've got paprika. You check. You have. Three.
  • The freezer stops being a mystery box.
  • The garage, the loft and the shed each get their own list.
  • Your collection is searchable, and you stop buying the game you already own.

At work

  • Stocktake stops being a clipboard and a lost afternoon. Walk the shelf, scan, tap +, and the count is in a spreadsheet before you sit down.
  • You can tell a customer what you've actually got, today, without going to look.
  • Your partner or a colleague works from the same list you do.

Built for homes and small operations — up to five places, up to five people. Not a warehouse system, and we'd rather say so now.

The part everyone gives up on

Three taps per thing.

Most inventory apps ask you to type. This one asks you to point.

Point the camera at a barcode. We search global product databases, and the name, description and photo come back filled in — in your language.

Then tap Save & scan next and keep going. The camera never closes. That's how you count a shelf.

  • Reads twelve barcode formats — everyday retail labels, QR codes and more.
  • Auto-zoom picks up small or distant barcodes, and a beep confirms every hit so you don't have to look.
  • No barcode? Add the item by hand.

Scan a barcode to add a new product or find existing one.

Once everything's in

Now find it again.

A list of everything you own is worth nothing if you can't get to one thing in it.

Search reads more than the name. Type any scrap you remember — half a barcode, a word from the description, the supplier you typed into a field of your own — and it finds the item.

Folder view turns each location into a folder. Tap Pantry, see what's in Pantry. Anything unfiled sits in Without location. Places side by side — there's no tree to build.

Your data, not our database

It's a Google Sheet. In your Drive. With your name on it.

The first time you sign in, 1inventory creates a spreadsheet in your own Google Drive. There's no setup step. Every product you add is a row.

It's a normal file. Open it on a laptop and sort it, chart it, run a formula over it, or share it with your accountant the way you'd share any Google file.

There's no export button, because there's nothing to export. Your data is already in the most portable format there is.

If we disappear

Same answer. That's how it's built, not just something we promise.

In plain words

The rest of your Drive is invisible to us.

When you sign in we ask for three things: your name, your email, and permission to create files in your Drive. That last one is the narrowest permission Google offers — it lets the app open the spreadsheet and photo folder it made for you, and nothing else. Google enforces that. It isn't a promise from us.

What it can open

  • The spreadsheet it created for you
  • The photo folder beside it
  • Your name, email and profile picture

What it can never see

  • Every other file in your Drive
  • Anything you didn't open with it
  • Gmail, Calendar, anything else in your Google account

Your products do pass through our servers on the way to Google. We don't keep them.

You can check it, and switch it off, any time in your Google Account under Security → Third-party apps.

Technically, that's the drive.file scope.
