1inventory is a barcode inventory app that saves to a Google Sheet you own.

We’d rather lose you here than in a one-star review.

The fastest way to make somebody distrust everything else on a website is to let them find out about a limitation after they have paid. So here is the whole list.

If any of it is a dealbreaker, close the tab with our blessing — and if you tell us which one, we will at least know. Nothing below is a roadmap. It is what the product does not do today, on the day this page was written.

Will it tell me when I’m running low?

No. Nothing in it knows how much of anything is left, except the number you last typed.

One kind of alert exists, and it watches a date. You add a date field of your own, you say how many days of warning you want, and the app emails you or pushes you a notification before the date arrives, or after it, if the thing is the kind of thing that slips. That is the whole automation story.

Nothing watches quantity. If your real job is selling things, buy a till system, and we mean that.

More on how the date alerts work, and what they cost, on the FAQ and in plans.

Limitations

Ten gaps, listed properly

Every one of these was checked against the shipping code.

No offline mode.

Every operation in the app is a network call. There is no cached-read mode, no offline queue and no local copy of your products on the phone. Cellars, lock-ups, underground car parks, vans, building sites and market pitches in fields are all places where this app will not load. It is the biggest functional gap in the product. We know. Other apps beat us on it outright. The app itself labels offline mode “Coming soon”, and that is a plan, not a date.

Nothing updates by itself.

Two people on the same inventory do not see each other’s changes as they happen. Saving writes to your Google Sheet. The other phone finds out when somebody pulls the list down to refresh it. There is no background refresh, no notification when a number moves, and nothing watching the spreadsheet for edits you make on a laptop.

No export button.

No CSV export, no PDF report, no download-my-data button and no share sheet. Our answer is architectural: your rows are already in a Google Sheet in your own Drive, and Google will export that file in whatever format you want. We think it is the better answer. It is not the same thing as a button, so it belongs on this list. What the file looks like is its own page.

No iPad, no web app, no desktop app.

Portrait phone only, on both platforms. Orientation is locked in code on every device, tablets included, and there is no tablet layout anywhere in the app. There is no browser client and no desktop build. Your data opens on a laptop through Google Sheets, which is a real partial answer. There is no 1inventory to run there.

One photo per product, compressed hard.

Exactly one image per product. No gallery, no second angle. Your phone shrinks it to at most 500 × 500 before it leaves, and our side re-encodes it as WebP at quality 30. Good enough to recognise a jar on a shelf.

There is no serial-number field, no valuation, no receipt storage and no report of any kind. This app helps you know what you own. It does not help you prove it.

Flat locations, and nothing in bulk.

A location is a name and a position in a list. No parent, no tree, no breadcrumbs and no sub-locations. There is no multi-select, no bulk delete, no bulk move and no bulk edit anywhere in the app: everything is done one product at a time. Two views exist, List and Folder. There is no grid.

The app reads barcodes. It cannot generate or print one, so there is no shelf-label workflow. Search covers the name, the barcode, the description and the fields you added yourself, and it does not cover the location name, which surprises people. Quantities are whole numbers from 0 to 9,999.

Two roles, and five people is the ceiling.

An inventory has an owner, and it has members. That is the entire permission model. There is no read-only guest, no per-location access, no approval step and no audit role. Everybody who is in can edit everything and delete anything.

The numbers are five people including you, five locations and five inventories, on the paid plan. None of them can be bought up. Inviting anybody at all requires the owner to be subscribed, and the inventory runs on the owner’s Google account and the owner’s daily allowance of barcode lookups, which everybody in it shares.

Google sign-in only.

There is one button on the login screen and it says Google. No email and password, no magic link, no QR code, no local-only mode. If you do not want a Google account attached to this, there is no version of this product for you. The permission we ask for is the narrowest one Google offers, which is a separate argument and does not change this one.

10,000 products in one inventory.

On both plans. It is not divided between them, and it is enforced: at the ceiling the app stops and returns Product limit reached (10000). You will almost certainly never see it. We print the number because a number is checkable.

One caution goes with it. The app loads an entire inventory into memory and searches it again on every keystroke, and nobody here has measured how that feels at ten thousand rows. Read the figure as a wall. It is not a performance claim.

Five languages.

English, German, Spanish, French and Portuguese, and that includes the alert emails and the product text that comes back from a barcode lookup. Anything else, and you get English.

Go and buy something else if this is you.

None of these is a gap we are working on. They are jobs this product was not built for.

  • Running a warehouse, or anything with bins, aisles, pick lists, receiving or cycle counts.
  • Running a shop with a till. Buy a POS.
  • Documenting a house or a business for insurance. Buy something built for evidence.
  • Managing IT assets with serial numbers, check-in and check-out, and depreciation.
  • Needing to be told when to reorder.
  • Working somewhere without a signal.
  • Needing more than five people on one list, or any read-only role.
  • Needing it on an iPad, or in a browser.
  • Unwilling to connect a Google account.
  • Food, medicine, warranties, services and renewals are all handled by the date alerts, which is a different job from reordering and is worth not confusing with it.
  • A household, a workshop, a club store or a two-to-five-person shared list is what this was built for, and the caps above are the shape of that decision.
