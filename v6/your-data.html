Your data

Three permissions. Here they are, in full.

1inventory asks Google for three things: your name, your email address, and a permission called drive.file.

drive.file is the narrowest Drive permission Google offers. An app holding it can open the files it created itself, and nothing else. So 1inventory can open the spreadsheet it made for you, and the folder its photos go into. Everything else in your Drive — every file you made yourself, every file somebody shared with you — sits outside what that permission reaches. Google enforces that boundary, and we could not widen it without sending you back to a consent screen to approve it.

Last updated 1 August 2026.

3 Google permissions requested. All three are named below.

What the app asks Google for

drive.file

The one that does the work. It covers files this app created: your inventory spreadsheet, and the folder its photos are uploaded to. It can read and change those two things. A spreadsheet you made yourself is not one of them, and the app has no way to ask for it later.

userinfo.profile

Your name and profile picture, so that anyone you share an inventory with sees a person rather than an account id.

userinfo.email

Your email address. It identifies your account, and it is where alert emails arrive if you switch them on.

The broad Drive scopes are never requested: drive, drive.readonly, drive.metadata and spreadsheets. None of them appears in our configuration, so none of them can be used.

After you sign in, our backend asks Google’s own tokeninfo endpoint which scopes were actually granted, compares the answer against the three above, and refuses to continue if any of them is missing. That check runs on every login, and it exists because a client application can claim anything at all about itself and the only way to know what Google really handed over is to go and ask Google.

Written out in full, each one is prefixed https://www.googleapis.com/auth/, so the first is https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file.

So what can it actually open?

Two lists. The second one is longer, which is the point.

1inventory can

  • Create your inventory spreadsheet
  • Create a folder for its photos
  • Read and edit those two things
  • Upload photos into that folder
  • List the revision history of that one file

1inventory cannot

  • See any other file in your Drive
  • Search your Drive
  • Open a document you made
  • Read your Gmail
  • Read your Calendar or your Contacts
  • Know that any of it is there

Nothing in the second list is a policy we wrote. It is what Google’s permission model does with an application that only ever asked for the files it made.

Read the permission screen before you tap Allow. We’ll wait.

Both of these take about ten seconds, and neither of them involves us.

  • Before you install: the consent screen names the permission when you tap Sign in with Google. It is Google’s screen, and it says the same thing this page says.
  • After you install: open your Google Account, then Security, then Third-party apps with account access. Everything 1inventory has been granted is listed there, and the control that revokes it is on the same page. You do not have to ask us, or tell us.

The sentence we won’t write.

Plenty of apps built the way this one is built will tell you that your data never touches them. That would be a good sentence to be able to write, and we can’t write it.

Your rows pass through our servers.

Every product you add travels through our servers on the way to your spreadsheet, and comes back the same way when you open the app. That is how the app reaches Drive at all. We do not retain any of it: there is no table in our database that holds your inventory, and the privacy policy said so long before this page existed. The accurate version is that we don’t keep your rows, not that we couldn’t read one in transit.

There is one thing we do keep, and it is the only product data we hold at all: a shared cache of public barcode lookups. When a retail barcode is scanned, the name, description and image that come back are stored, so the next person who scans that barcode does not spend one of their lookups for the day. The cache is identical for every user and it contains nothing of yours.

The legal version of everything on this page is in the privacy policy.

If you delete your account, the spreadsheets stay

Deleting your 1inventory account

It removes your inventories and their settings from the app. On the same screen there is a separate checkbox offering to delete the Google Sheets and the images from your Drive as well, and it starts unticked. You would have to go looking for it. Leave it alone and the vendor goes while the data stays.

Deleting the app

The spreadsheet is in your Drive tonight, and next year, and after you have changed phones twice. You lose the scanner. You keep the file.

What that file actually contains, tab by tab and column by column, is set out on the sheet. The things this app will not do for you are listed on what it doesn’t do, and the permission questions people send us are answered on the FAQ.

Install it, then go and look at your Drive.

Sign in with Google and scan one thing. Then open drive.google.com on a laptop: there will be a spreadsheet in there created a minute ago, with that thing in it.
