This is the file, and this is exactly what’s in it.

When you create an inventory in 1inventory, the app creates a real Google Spreadsheet in your own Google Drive and uses it as the database. Your products are rows in that file. You own it, you can open it on any laptop, and it stays if the app goes away.

Info

Written once, when the sheet is created, in your language. It welcomes you to the sheet, links back to 1inventory.io, and sets out two rules:

  • Don’t change the names of the columns the app made, and don’t move them.
  • Don’t rename or delete any of the tabs.

It also tells you where your product images live, hyperlinked to the actual folder on your Drive. That line is the only place in the entire product that says where the photos went.

Products

Seven columns before you add any of your own.

  • A · Name. The only required field.
  • B · Quantity. Whole numbers, 0 to 9,999 in the app.
  • C · Barcode. Free text, optional, and nothing validates it.
  • D · Location. Matches the name of one of your locations.
  • E · Description. Free text.
  • F · Image. A link into the images folder on your Drive.
  • G · Last Updated. Written by the server.

How it’s set up

The header row is bold, centred and frozen. A basic filter covers A to G and widens as you add fields. Quantity is number-formatted as an integer. Your own fields append to the right as H, I, J and onwards.

History

Seven columns: the product, its barcode, what happened to it, the quantity before and after, who did it, and when.

What the app writes, and what it leaves alone

When the app updates a row it writes the value and the formatting. That is all it writes. There is a comment in the code next to that decision explaining why: writing the whole row back would also wipe the notes and the data validation you added by hand.

So carry on using the file as a spreadsheet. Type a note into a cell we don’t manage and it survives. Add data validation to a column and it survives. Leave a blank spacer row between two sections and the app steps over it instead of reading it as a product with no name.

It writes the cell it changed. Nothing else on the sheet moves.

Go ahead and edit it yourself

  • Add a column. Type a header the app has never heard of and its values come back to the app as a custom field.
  • Move a column. Drag it somewhere else in Google Sheets and the app follows it. That is in fact the only way to reorder your own fields, because there are no drag handles in the app. The seven columns the app made are the exception: those stay where they are.

Type a location straight into column D and the app notices. You get a green link reading View 3 unmapped locations, and a sheet that explains itself: “These location values are used in your products but haven’t been created yet. Add them to organize your inventory.” Each one gets an Add button, green if you have a location slot free and orange if it needs an upgrade.

That screen only exists because we genuinely expect you to open the file and type into it, which is a thing no inventory app keeping your catalogue on its own servers would ever have to build, and it is the clearest evidence we can give you that the spreadsheet is the database.

So what happens if you break it?

Rename or move one of the seven columns and the app stops, then tells you exactly which columns to add or rename. Delete the History tab and it notices on the next read, builds the tab again with the right headings and formatting, and retries. Recreating a tab does not bring back what was in it. For that you want the next bit.

Backups

Three steps, and all three are Google’s

Your inventory is a Google Sheet, so it inherits Google Drive’s own revision history. The Backups screen in the app lists the revision timestamps it can see and then hands you over: open the sheet from the link, go to File → Version history → See version history, pick the version you want and click Restore.

There is no restore button in 1inventory. The restoring happens inside Google Sheets, which is the honest description and also the more reassuring one, because it means it does not depend on us.

If somebody invited you, the file isn’t yours to open.

The spreadsheet lives in the inventory owner’s Drive, not in yours. You can use the app exactly as they do, but the Open in Google Sheet link never appears for you anywhere, because every read and write runs under the owner’s Google account and no Drive sharing is performed.

  • The things it deliberately does not do are listed on What it doesn’t do, and the limits per plan are on Plans.
  • Anything still unanswered is probably in the FAQ.

Open it on a laptop before you trust us

Install the app, sign in with Google, scan five things off one shelf, then open the file on a laptop. If it is not the file we described, you will know in ten minutes.
