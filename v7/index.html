The flow

How it works, from empty to up to date.

  1. 1

    You create an inventory

    Name it whatever you like and the app builds a Google Spreadsheet for it in your own Drive.

  2. 2

    You add your own columns

    Expiry date, supplier, serial number, warranty end. Add whatever the built-in columns don’t cover, in eight field types, and each one becomes a real column in the sheet.

  3. 3

    You scan it in

    The camera reads the barcode and the name, photo and description arrive filled in. Choose which location it lives in, save, and the product exists.

  4. 4

    You find it again

    Scan the barcode a second time and it opens that product, or type any part of the name.

  5. 5

    You change the number

    The quantity it already has comes straight up. Thumb it up or down, save, move on.

  6. 6

    It tells you in time

    Put an expiry, warranty or service date on a product and the app pushes or emails you before it passes. This is the one step you do nothing for.

How it works

Point. Confirm. Next.

Tap the green circle, point the camera at a barcode, and the app searches global product databases. The name, description and photo come back filled in, in your language. Tap Save & scan next and the camera is already back up.

There is no hardware to buy. No scanner gun, nothing to pair, nothing to charge. It reads twelve barcode formats, zooms itself in on small print, and beeps so you can keep your eyes on the shelf.

  • No barcode? Add it by hand in a few seconds.
  • Own-brand and unlabelled stock scans fine and comes back blank, and you type the name.
  • Looking a barcode up costs one of 5 free lookups a day, or 200 on Premium. Scanning itself is never metered.

Once everything’s in

Then find it again.

Every week after that

The second scan is a stocktake.

Scan something you already own and the app doesn’t ask you anything. It shows the number you had and waits. Thumb it up or down, save, and the camera comes back. Walk the shelf and the count is done before you sit down.

  • Alerts. Put an expiry, warranty or service date on something and it tells you days before, by push or email.
  • History. What changed, who did it and when. Premium.
  • Dashboard. Products, locations, total stock and last activity on one screen.

Nothing decrements on its own. There’s no till feed and no low-stock alert. If you sell things all day, this count will drift.

Your data, not our database

It’s a Google Sheet. In your Drive.

The first time you sign in, 1inventory creates a real spreadsheet in your own Google Drive. Every product you add is a row in it. Open it on a laptop, sort it, chart it, or share it with your accountant the way you’d share any Google file.

Stop paying and nothing is deleted. Delete the app and the file is still in your Drive. If we disappeared tomorrow, you would still have everything, because it was never ours to begin with.

There’s no export button, because there’s nothing to export.

In plain words

The rest of your Drive is invisible to us.

We ask for your name, your email, and permission to create files in your Drive. That last one is the narrowest permission Google offers, and Google enforces it — it isn’t a promise from us.

What it can open

  • The spreadsheet it made for you
  • The photo folder beside it

What it can never see

  • Every other file in your Drive
  • Gmail, Calendar, anything else

Your products pass through our servers on the way to Google. We don’t keep them. Technically, that’s the drive.file scope.
